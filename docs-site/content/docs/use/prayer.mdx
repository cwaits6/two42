## Open the prayer page

Choose **Prayer** in the menu. The page shows your group's requests, newest first, and a **Share a request** box. Only members of the group can see its requests.

Use **Search requests…** to find a request by its text or by the name on it. Use the **All**, **My requests** and **Answered** buttons to narrow the list.

## Post a request

1. In **Share a request**, type your request. A request can be up to 2,000 characters.
2. To post without your name, turn on **Share anonymously**.
3. Check the line under the box. It reads **Posting to** your group **as** your name, or as **Anonymous**.
4. Select **Post request**.

## Who sees your name

On a request posted with your name, people in the group see your name and photo when they can see you in the directory. Your name shows with its suffix, such as Jr., when you've added one to your profile, on requests and comments alike. Anyone who can't, for example because you're hidden from the directory, sees **A member** instead. See [Directory](/docs/use/directory) for what decides who appears there.

On an anonymous request, everyone sees **Anonymous** and no photo. That includes the group's leaders and the church admins: no page in two42 shows them who posted it. You see a **You** badge on your own anonymous requests, so you can still mark them answered or delete them.

<Callout type="info">
Request text is stored encrypted, and an anonymous request stores no name, only a coded value that the server can match to you. The people who run your church's two42 server hold the keys, so they could work out who posted an anonymous request. The details are in [Prayer request encryption](https://github.com/cwaits6/two42/blob/main/docs/security/prayer-encryption.md).
</Callout>

## Pray for a request

Select **I'm praying** on a request. The button changes to **You're praying** and the count of people praying goes up by one. Select it again to take your name off the count. The count shows how many people are praying, not who.

## Mark your request answered

On your own request, select **Mark answered**. The request gets an **Answered** badge and shows under the **Answered** filter. Select **Answered** again to undo it.

## Delete your request

1. On your own request, select **Delete**.
2. Select **Delete request** to confirm.

The request and its comments are gone for everyone in the group, and can't be brought back. Only the person who posted a request sees **Delete** on it.

## Comment on a request

1. Select **Comment** under a request.
2. Type your comment in **Add a comment**. A comment can be up to 500 characters; a count appears when you're close to the limit.
3. Choose who sees it and whose name is on it:
   - **Only … can see this**, where the switch names the person who asked: only you and the person who posted the request can read the comment. Others in the group, leaders and church admins included, don't see it. The comment shows a **Private** badge. This choice isn't offered on your own request.
   - **Hide my name**: the comment shows as **Anonymous** to everyone, including leaders and church admins. On your own anonymous request this starts on, so a reply doesn't give away that you asked.
4. Select **Post comment**.

Nobody can edit a comment after posting it.

## Delete a comment

1. Select **Delete** under the comment.
2. Select **Delete comment** to confirm.

You can delete your own comments and any comment on your own requests. The group's leaders can delete any comment in the group.

## How long requests are kept

Requests stay until the person who posted them deletes them, unless a church admin has chosen a time limit. The **Old prayer requests** setting in **Church settings** can delete requests 6 months, 1 year or 2 years after they were posted, counted from the day they were posted. Marking a request answered doesn't give it more time. See [Church admin pages](/docs/administer/church-admin).

When you delete your account, your prayer requests and comments go with it, including anonymous ones. See [Delete your account](/docs/use/delete-account).
