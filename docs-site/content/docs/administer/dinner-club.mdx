A dinner club is a group's dinners in members' homes, run as **seasons**. Members sign up as a **party** of one or two, a leader shuffles the parties into **tables**, and each table has a **host** whose home it meets at for each **dinner**. A church that wants a dinner club for everyone makes a group for it and uses the same page.

## Turn it on

In **Group settings › Details**, turn on **Dinner club** and save. The group's menu gains **Dinner club**, after Serving. It starts off for every group. Turning it off hides the page and keeps every season, so turning it on again brings them back.

## Add a season

On the Dinner club page, choose **Add season** and fill in:

- **Title**: your own name for it, such as "Supper Club".
- **Format**, one per season:
  - **Same table, one host**: the tables stay the same all season, and each table's host party hosts every dinner.
  - **Same table, hosts take turns**: the tables stay the same, and the parties at each table who can host take turns.
  - **New tables each dinner**: a new shuffle for every dinner, from the hosts free that night. Nobody hosts two dinners in a row unless you allow it.
- **Start date** and **Length**, as a number of months or an end date.
- **How often**: every few weeks on a day, or monthly on a weekday such as the 2nd Saturday, and a **Start time**.
- **Table size**: the fewest and most people at a table, 6 to 8 to start.
- **Sign-ups close**: the last day members can sign up or change their sign-up.
- **Hosts say what each guest brings**: on to start. Turn it off for a season whose hosts cook everything. You can change it after publishing.

The dinner dates are proposed from these. On the season's page you can **Add date**, **Move date** and **Delete date**, before and after publishing, and **Propose dates again** before publishing. A date whose dinners have happened can't be deleted. A season starts as a draft that members don't see; **Delete season** removes a draft.

Only one season per group takes sign-ups or runs at a time.

## Sign-ups

Choose **Open sign-ups**. Members then see the season on the Dinner club page and sign up alone, with someone in their household, or with anyone else in the group. A person is in one party per season. A party who can host gives their seats (their own party included) and the address for the dinners, which starts from their household's directory address. Depending on the format they can say which date they'd like to host or which dates they can't. Anyone can add their allergies and food needs. Kids aren't seated: a party is the adults coming.

The **Sign-ups** list on the season's page shows each party, the number of parties, hosts, people and seats, and a warning when there are too few hosts: people divided by the most per table, rounded up, for each dinner when tables change each dinner, and for the season otherwise. **Add party** signs someone up, with one other person if they're coming together, and **Remove party** takes a party off while sign-ups are open.

A member can **Withdraw** while sign-ups are open, and **Withdraw from this season** after they close.

## Shuffle and publish

On the season's page choose **Make tables**, then **Shuffle**. The shuffle keeps each party together, gives every table a host, keeps each table within its host's seats (the fewest seats of its hosts when they take turns), evens out the table sizes, and avoids leaving one single person alone among couples. When tables change each dinner it also tries to keep apart people who already ate together this season; when the table stays the same, its people eat together at every dinner. In a season started from an earlier one, it also keeps apart people who shared a table in the last two seasons, after its other rules: with enough people, nobody sits with last season's tablemates. The draft is only for leaders. Change it with **Shuffle again**, **Move or swap** on a party, or **Host** on a table. Warnings show a table over its seats, a dinner with no host, a repeat pairing and a host with two dinners in a row.

**Publish tables** shows each member their own table and emails each person their dinners, dates and host. After publishing you can change the tables and choose **Publish changes**; only the people whose table or host changed get an email. A dinner that has happened keeps its table and host, so once the first dinners have happened, change later tables by hand, not with **Shuffle again**. Emails count toward the church's daily email limit; if it is reached, the tables are still published and no email goes out.

## Dinners

Each table's dinner takes its date's time. Its host can **Move dinner** to another date and time within the season, and a leader can move any table's dinner. Moving a dinner emails the people at that table, except the person who moved it, and clears its replies so people answer again. Moving a season date moves each table's dinner on it, except one already moved on its own.

Each person answers **Going** or **Not going** for each of their dinners, and can answer for their partner. The host sees who is going, the counts, and the food needs of the parties coming. Replies stop once the dinner starts.

The day before each dinner, everyone at the table who hasn't said Not going gets a reminder with the time, host, address and who's coming. A person who turns off **Email me about dinner club dinners** in Settings gets no dinner club email.

## When plans change

The season's page shows **Needs attention** when a host can't host a dinner, a host has withdrawn, or a party is waiting for a table, with the tables that have room. The Dinner club page shows a leader the same in one line. **Open tables** goes to the tables, where each change is made in the draft and sent with **Publish changes**. Only the people whose table or host changed get an email.

**A host can't host one dinner.** The host chooses **Can't host this dinner** on their dinner. The table shows which dinner, and what you can do depends on the format:

- **Same table, one host**: choose **Host on** that date to have another party at the table host that one dinner, or **Move dinner** in the list of published dinners. A party that didn't offer to host needs **Add hosting details** first, once they've agreed: their seats and the address for the dinners. Once a stand-in is published, the table shows **Host on** for each dinner, so changing one dinner's host leaves the others alone.
- **Same table, hosts take turns**: **Swap turns with** another date's host at the table, or pick another host for that date.
- **New tables each dinner**: **Move guests** spreads the table's guests across that night's other tables with room, couples first, keeping apart people who already ate together where it can. The host is left off that night and listed under **Waiting for a table** for that date. When a guest would have no table with room, or it is the only table that night, nobody moves: move or swap guests by hand, or move the dinner.

Moving the dinner asks its host again.

**A party withdraws.** Their seats at dinners still to come are freed, and dinners that have happened keep them. After publishing, **Remove party** on the season's page does the same. When the one host of a table withdraws, its dinners still to come have no host until you choose one with **Host** on the table, or **Add hosting details** for a party there who agrees to take it on.

**Waiting list and late parties.** After publishing, a member who isn't signed up can **Join the waiting list**, and **Add party** on the season's page adds a party to it. On the tables page, **Waiting for a table** lists them: **Seat at** puts a party at a table with room and changes no other table, so only that table gets an email when you publish. When tables change each dinner, seat them for each date. A waiting party sees no table, address or list until seated. They can't add food needs after sign-ups close, so they tell you.

## What guests bring

When the season has **Hosts say what each guest brings** on, the host of a dinner chooses **What guests bring** on it, and a leader does the same from the list of published dinners. Pick Side, Salad, Dessert, Drinks, Bread, **Something else** with your own words, or **Nothing** for each guest party, then **Save list**. **Suggest** fills the list for you to change: a different thing for each guest, and for each party something other than what they brought last time. Each guest sees **You're bringing** on their dinner, and the reminder email says it.

## End and start the next season

When the last dinner is done, choose **End season**. Members keep seeing their tables; replies, moves and dinner emails stop. An ended season can't be changed.

On an ended season, **Start next season** opens **Add season** with the same format, how often, start time, table size and settings, starting the day after its end date and running as long. Add a title and when sign-ups close. When you open sign-ups, last season's parties see **Sign up again** with their own answers filled in, to change before they sign up. Their address, food needs and home notes carry over only when the same people sign up together.

## Who sees what

- **A host's address**: the host, the group's leaders, and the people seated at that host's table, only after tables are published.
- **Allergies and food needs**: the person, their partner, the group's leaders, and the host of a dinner they're at.
- **Tables**: each member sees only their own. Leaders see every table.
- **What guests bring**: each guest sees what their own party brings; the host and the group's leaders see the whole list for that dinner. Nobody sees another table's list.
- **The waiting list**: a waiting party sees no table, address or list until they're seated.

A church admin who isn't in the group sees none of it.

Dinners never appear on the group calendar, on Home, or in the group's calendar subscription. Each person has their own **Calendar link** on the Dinner club page for their own dinners, with times and addresses. **Reset link** ends the old one, for example if it was shared.
